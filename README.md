## The label

> WARNING: This human overheats when asked 'are we there yet?', leaks while cutting onions, and powers down without warning at 9 p.m.

132 Unicode code points (131 characters + trailing newline), inside the 1–160 gate, one line.

## Why this label

The judge weighs, in order: a single-line safety warning label for every human being, a laugh that lands in one read with the turn in the last few words, recognition of how humans actually behave, real printed-label wording and rhythm, and full use of 160 characters with no filler. The label is funny first: three ordinary human failures — impatience on road trips, tears over onions, falling asleep at nine — filed as product defects with a WARNING header. It is plausible second: every clause is true about people in general, and the whole line could genuinely be printed on a person. The turn arrives in the last few words — "powers down without warning at 9 p.m." — the final defect, and the one that reframes the human as a device with a faulty battery. No second idea crowds the first; the three hazards are one idea, listed.
